# DMCA & Copyright

Nocturne is a transparent web platform — it does not host, store, or serve copyrighted content. All traffic originates from third-party websites requested by the user.

## Role of the Operator

Nocturne operates as a **mere conduit** under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 512(a)). The operator does not select, modify, or retain the content that flows through the service. As such, takedown requests are typically more appropriate to direct to the original host of the material.

> **Before submitting a notice:** please verify the infringing content is actually hosted on Nocturne (e.g., `/src/`, `/public/` assets, or HTML served directly by this domain). Content reached *through* the service lives on third-party servers — not here.

## Submitting a Notice

If you are a copyright owner and believe material hosted on this domain infringes your rights, send a written notice to the operator of this Nocturne instance including:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed.
3. The exact URL on this domain where the material resides.
4. Your contact information (name, address, phone, email).
5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized.
6. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that the information in the notice is accurate and that you are authorized to act on behalf of the copyright owner.

## Counter-Notice

If you believe content was removed in error, you may submit a counter-notice with equivalent information and a statement, under penalty of perjury, of good-faith belief that the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.

## Repeat Infringers

If the operator receives multiple valid takedown notices for the same infringing source, the relevant content will be removed at the service level. Nocturne does not maintain account bans or IP blocklists, but persistent abuse may result in the affected destinations being filtered for everyone.

## Misuse

Knowingly submitting a false DMCA notice may subject you to liability under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), including damages and attorney's fees.
